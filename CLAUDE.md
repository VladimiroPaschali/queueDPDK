# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this repo is

Two DPDK applications whose throughput is measured while varying the number of RX queues polled per core (1 to 2048) and the queue-selection policy, plus the scripts that re-run every test of the paper. The target hardware is an AMD Alveo U55N running a modified Open-NIC shell with the QDMA PMD. Comments are partly in Italian. `README.md` has the setup (DPDK tree and patch, bitstream, host) and the table figure → step → results → plot.

- `nf-chain/`: the paper's "NF chain", an ACL followed by a Count-Min Sketch and a NAT. The NAT code lives in `src/nat_*`, `state.*` and `utils/`. The ACL comes from l3fwd-acl and is driven by `rules/fw_10k` and `rules/rule_ipv6.db`.
- `mica/`: the paper's MICA key-value store. `ported-mica/` is the MICA table, and the workload alternates GET and SET. Five builds (`mica/Makefile`), all with true FIFO eviction, no copy of the 1400-byte value into the RX buffer and MICA's request pipeline (prefetch bucket, then item), plus two changes of the pipeline that are on by default (`MICA_HT=1` in `mica/Makefile`): the packet headers of the burst are prefetched before the keys are hashed (`MICA_PF_HDR`, `main.c`) and the bucket tags are compared as one AVX-512 vector instead of a loop with a branch per slot (`MICA_TAGMASK`, `ported-mica/table.c`; the host CPU needs AVX-512, the Makefile uses `-march=native`). `make clean && make MICA_HT=0` builds the MICA of before 9 Nov 2026 (the NDR results of MICA tagged `pfch64` from before that date are of that build, with Aggressive+Penalty): `mica-fifo-nocopy-prefetch` (single core), `-pools-prefetch` (one log pool per lcore, Partitioned), `-prefetch-lat` (latency.py), `-prefetch-qprobe` (starvation.py), `-prefetch-adaptive` (adaptive.py). The `-lat`, `-qprobe` and `-adaptive` builds copy nothing into the packet (`MICA_KEEP_PAYLOAD`), so the TRex latency trailer comes back intact. The names are historical (they are in the results folders and in the paper's source).
- `common/qflow.h`: CLI options, validation and names of the policies, shared by every app. `common/qbitstream.h`: which Open-NIC bitstream is loaded. Every variant keeps `0x0940907F` in user-BAR register 0x0, so after that check every app prints `bitstream: <name> (...)` from the depths in config-BAR registers 0xBE0 (C2H prefetch cache) and 0xBE4 (completion coalescing buffer): `extended_table` (16/16, the original bitstream, every result before 24 Oct 2026, no longer in the repo), `pfch64` (64-queue prefetch cache: `extended_table_prefetch.bit`, the bitstream of the paper), `coal32`, `pfch64-coal32`. `no-drop-zip.py` copies the line into its log. `common/qprobe.h` (poll timestamps and marked packets of the starvation runs), `common/adaptive.h` and `common/qdma_rss.[ch]` (adaptive queue count, below) are included by the app variants.
- `extended_table_prefetch.{bit,mcs}`: the bitstream (`sudo ./program_fpga.sh` loads it; `.mcs` = flash image, made with Vivado as README.md says). With 16 prefetch entries the NIC receive ceiling depends only on the active RX queues (17 Mpps at 16 queues, 10 at 32, 7.2 at 2048, whatever the cores: one PCIe descriptor read per packet once more than ~8-16 queues share the cache), which made multicore curves show the NIC; pfch64 lifts it (30 Mpps up to 32 queues, 22.7 at 64, 13-16 from 128). `patches/dpdk-20.11-qdma.patch` is the whole difference between DPDK 20.11 + the Open-NIC-patched QDMA driver and the tree the apps link against (`../../dpdk-20.11`: 2048 queues, user-register access, the RETA that writes the RSS mask, RX doorbell every 32 packets).
- `experiments/`: the scripts. `run_all.sh [--no-program] [--dry-run] [--apps ...] [step ...]` runs program → locality → ndr, latency, starvation, adaptive (MICA, then nf-chain) → plot, through `run_locality.sh`, `run_ndr.sh <app>`, `run_latency.sh <app>`, `run_starvation.sh <app>`, `run_adaptive.sh <app>` and `plot_all.sh`; `lib.sh` is what they share (`TAG=pfch64`, `run` echoes and honours `DRY_RUN`, the app → experiment names). MICA is measured with Round-Robin and Aggressive on 1 core and Aggressive on 2 and 4 cores (`run_ndr.sh`), its adaptive runs are `mica-flows-rate8` and `mica-ramp-max9` against 1 and 128 fixed queues with `--adaptive-search`, and its latency sweep goes in 0.25 Mpps steps with `--stop-loss 5`, the panel loads 1.8, 4.6 and 6.2 Mpps always measured and 6.2 three times (`run_latency.sh`); nf-chain keeps Round-Robin, Penalty and Aggressive+Penalty (Aggressive+Penalty on 2 and 4 cores), the two adaptive variants against 1, 16, 128 and 2048 queues, and the sweep in 0.1 Mpps steps. Results are tagged with the bitstream name: `results/ndr/results_<experiment>-pfch64_skew*`.
- `experiments/no-drop-zip.py`: zero-loss throughput (RFC2544 NDR) with TRex on another host (`STLClient(server="100.78.72.16")`). Traffic comes from the `zipf-profile.py` profile. After the NDR search the script keeps the same app process running, has TRex send at the NDR rate and takes `perf stat -C <lcores>` windows (plus system-wide IMC CAS counts for DRAM bytes), normalized by the packets TRex sent in each window. It disables turbo (`cpufreq/boost`) for the run, and `--resume` skips points already in the CSVs. `EXPERIMENTS` has 8 entries: `nf-chain`, `nf-chain-partitioned{2,4}`, `nf-chain-seq-ring8192`, `mica-fifo-nocopy-prefetch`, `mica-fifo-nocopy-pools-prefetch-partitioned{2,4}`, `mica-fifo-nocopy-prefetch-seq-trex2`. The `-seq-*` experiments give the "theoretical max", the bound of a perfect policy: `sequential-profile.py` sends the same Zipf flows one after the other (trains proportional to the Zipf weight, at least 32 packets, chained single-burst streams) on 1 queue, with TRex pinned to one core (`trex_core_mask`; two cores for MICA, whose one-core rate is below what MICA takes), since TRex splits a burst over its cores and would interleave the flows again. nf-chain's uses `-d 8192`, the largest RX ring the QDMA accepts: on 1 queue the 1024-descriptor ring, not the processing, limits the zero-loss rate of sequential traffic. `plot_ndr.py` plots throughput and the per-packet events against the queue count (and, for the multicore, one line per core count), `plot_bound.py` draws the bound as "theoretical max" and round robin on 1 queue as "baseline" over the policies (`--tag pfch64 --lines-tagged`). `pqos.py` collects LLC/memory-bandwidth data for the Discussion section.
- `experiments/apps/starvation/`: nf-chain plus `common/qprobe.h`, which timestamps every poll, logs the packets marked by `starvation-profile.py` (field-engine seq on sampled Zipf flows, TRex latency trailer on probes and finite flows) and prints per-queue drain and service gaps. `starvation.py` runs the starvation/FCT campaign (round robin and aggressive, 2 Mpps, skew 0.6, a 1024-packet flow at 200 kpps on top) and `plot_starvation.py` plots it. TRex splits a field-engine variable across its 4 DP cores by interleaving (core j sends j, j+4, ...), so per-flow seq analysis groups by `seq % k`.
- `experiments/apps/adaptive/`: single-core nf-chain that picks the number of active RX queues at runtime (`--adaptive`, see `common/adaptive.h`). All `-q` queues (a power of 2) are set up at startup. The app then rewrites the Open-NIC RSS mask register (`queues-1`, the first indirection-table entry that the patched `qdma_dev_reta_update` writes; `common/qdma_rss.c` computes its address from the PMD's private `func_id`, so the Makefile adds the QDMA driver's private include dirs) and polls only the active queues, draining the dropped ones after a shrink. The controller (`adaptive_epoch()`) doubles the queues when more than `--adaptive-backlog-high` percent of the full polls leave over half the ring waiting (the backlog is read in O(1) from the PMD's completion indexes, `qdma_rx_backlog()` in `common/qdma_rss.c`; on the FPGA this share tracks loss), and halves them below `--adaptive-backlog-low` percent or when the sweeps that found work have more than `--adaptive-empty-high` empty polls; a grow that raises cycles per packet without lowering the backlog is undone, and on flaps it keeps the larger count. Full polls alone are not a signal: Aggressive drains a backlog with full polls without losing anything. Every second the app prints `poll mix:` (empty and full polls of the active queues in the sweeps that found work, busy fraction, largest backlog), also without `--adaptive`. No multicore. `--latency-queue` follows the latency flow as the active queues change (its queue with N queues is its queue with -q queues & (N-1)). MICA has the same loop and controller in `mica/main.c` under `MICA_ADAPTIVE` (build `mica-fifo-nocopy-prefetch-adaptive`; `adaptive.py --app mica` with the `mica-*` scenarios). `adaptive.py` runs it while TRex changes rate, skew, flows and packet size phase by phase (the four paper scenarios in `SCENARIOS`: `ramp` and `flows-rate35` for nf-chain, `mica-ramp-max9` (3 to 9 Mpps) and `mica-flows-rate8` for MICA; `mica-ramp` and `mica-flows-rate7`, nf-chain's rates doubled, are for the build with `MICA_HT=0`; every run loads the TRex streams before the app starts, so the phases of the runs of a scenario begin together), compares it with fixed queue counts and logs a per-second timeline; `plot_adaptive.py` plots it. Every restart of the TRex traffic costs a transient (the first packet of every flow at once plus a loss that grows with rate and queue count), so `adaptive.py` changes flows and rate on the fly instead of restarting. `apps/adaptive/signal-policy.md` (Italian) describes the controller. With 32 or more RX queues the NIC drops 25-60 packets per second whatever the rate (0.002-0.004% at 1-3 Mpps, none with 1-16 queues; real drops, they never arrive late), well below the 0.1% loss threshold of the NDR searches.
- `experiments/apps/locality/`: a separate CMS app plus `locality-bench.py` for Fig. 1c (distinct flows per burst). It does not use the policies.

## Build

```
cd nf-chain && make                       # build/nf-chain
cd mica && make                           # the five builds above
cd experiments/apps/starvation && make    # build/starvation
cd experiments/apps/adaptive && make      # build/adaptive
cd experiments/apps/locality && make      # build/cms
```

The apps call QDMA PMD functions directly (`qdma_reg_read_usr`, `rte_pmd_qdma_*`). They can only be linked statically against the patched tree `../../dpdk-20.11` (`DPDK_LOCAL_PATH`; `../../../../dpdk-20.11` for `experiments/apps/*`). A `pkg-config` shared build fails at link time. There are no unit tests. Running on the FPGA is the only end-to-end check. Without the FPGA you can still smoke-test argument parsing: `sudo` is not needed with `--no-huge -m 256 --no-pci --vdev=net_null0 -l 0 -- <args>`. The run then crashes at the QDMA bitstream check, which is expected, and `stdbuf -oL` keeps the output.

## Policies and the polling loop

Policies are runtime options (see the table at the top of `common/qflow.h`): `--policy baseline|aggressive|penalty|aggressive+penalty`, `--penalty N`, `--latency-queue Q`, `--latency-period P` and `--multicore partitioned` (the only multicore scheme: Shared, in which every lcore polled every queue under a lock, was removed after it never beat Partitioned). In the code, Penalty is the variable `penalty` with the counters `queue_hit[]`, and Aggressive is `aggressive` with `max_loops`.

Each app has a single `poll_loop(const enum qflow_mode mode)` marked `__rte_always_inline`. It is instantiated twice (`main_loop_single`, `main_loop_partitioned`), so each mode compiles to its own loop. The two modes intentionally keep the internals of the original separate programs, so the paper's numbers stay reproducible. Do not "unify" these differences without re-measuring:

- **Single core.** TX on queue 0 per burst, per-second `print_stats()`, and throughput measured after a 3 s warmup.
- **Multicore.** TX is batched (`TX_BATCH_SIZE`) on the lcore's own TX queue, with no per-second statistics.
- **Penalty counters.** Global on a single core, per lcore otherwise. Partitioned indexes them relative to its slice and keeps that index across a latency diversion.
- **nf-chain CMS.** 16×1M with a row-pointer table on a single core, 16×65536 embedded in Partitioned, one sketch per lcore.
- **nf-chain NAT.** One table, one per lcore (Partitioned). Multicore drops new flows when the table is full.
- **nf-chain mempool.** One per lcore in Partitioned, a single one otherwise.
- **mica.** Non-concurrent table with FIFO eviction on a single core. With more than one polling lcore the table is concurrent, and multicore uses an MTH threshold of 1.0. The single-core mempool omits the per-lcore cache term. Partitioned splits queues with `queues/lcores + extra` (nf-chain uses `queues*pos/lcores`). The `-pools` build gives every polling lcore its own log pool: with one pool, the lock every SET takes while writing the 1400-byte value made 2 cores slower than 1.

The Python scripts parse the app output with regexes (`measured RX Throughput:`, `empty/sec (the poll read no packets)`, `pkts/burst`, `bitstream: <name>`, and `ready` / `entering main loop` for startup). Keep those strings stable.

## Running

Use the FPGA at PCI `0000:16:00.1`, loading the PMD with `-d librte_net_qdma.so`. `ports.sh` rebinds the device and sets the Open-NIC registers (after a reboot 16:00.0 has memory decoding off, so it first sets it with `setpci`; a readback of `0xFFFFFFFF` means the writes were lost). `sudo ./program_fpga.sh [file.bit]` loads a bitstream over JTAG (xsdb; default `extended_table_prefetch.bit`), removes and rescans the PCIe functions and then does what `ports.sh` does. Since 16 Oct 2026 the host boots with `isolcpus=0-11 nohz_full=0-11 rcu_nocbs=0-11 nosmt` (16 CPUs; the kernel keeps the tick on CPU 0, so `nohz_full` covers 1-11); the results measured before (every untagged `results/ndr/results_*` of the original paper) ran with SMT on and no isolated cores. The EAL takes 20 1 GB pages from `/dev/hugepages`, remounted with `pagesize=1G`. nf-chain needs `-p 0x3 --config="(0,0,<lcore>)" --rule_ipv4=... --rule_ipv6=...`, and MICA takes only `-q`, `-d`, `-v` and the policy options. MICA allocates its table from 2 MB hugepages in `/mnt/huge`, which must be a `hugetlbfs` mount with `pagesize=2M` and about 4096 free 2 MB pages (otherwise it dies with SIGBUS at `initializing pages`); files of killed runs keep the pages (`rm /mnt/huge/mehcached_shm_*`). Neither the mount nor the pages survive a reboot. At startup both apps send `log2(queues)+1` packets carrying a magic value to clear leftover state. A packet with the `STOP123456789` magic stops nf-chain.

Experiments: `cd experiments && python3 no-drop-zip.py <experiment>...`, or the step scripts through `run_all.sh`, in tmux with `trap : INT` (the `run-campaign` skill). `--queues/--policies/--skews/--repetitions` override an experiment and `--tag=-x` (the `=` is needed) writes `results_<experiment>-x_skew*`, so a subset never overwrites the main CSVs (without `--resume` the CSV of the folder is deleted). For the latency test (Fig. 6) `latency.py` runs `nf-chain -q <Q> --policy ... --latency-queue <probe queue>` while TRex measures the latency of a probe flow; the probe's queue depends on the NIC hash and is cached in `latency_queues.json` (found again by `run_latency.sh` once per bitstream).

Layout of `experiments/`: the scripts at the top; `apps/`; `results/ndr/results_<experiment>_skew*` (no-drop-zip.py, sequential runs included), `results/latency/`, `results/starvation/`, `results/adaptive/`, `results/locality/`; `plots/ndr/`, `plots/bound/`, `plots/latency/`, `plots/starvation/`, `plots/adaptive/` (every plot script writes there, not into the results folders); `logs/` for the campaign logs. The scripts default to these folders. Results (`*.csv`, `experiments/results/`), plots, `perf.data` and `build/` are gitignored.

The old campaigns (Shared, Marco's PMD, the `qceil/` ceiling tool, CAT, the A/B variants of MICA, adaptive NDR) are not in the repo any more; they are in `~/dpdk_patched/queueDPDK-archive/` and in the git history.
